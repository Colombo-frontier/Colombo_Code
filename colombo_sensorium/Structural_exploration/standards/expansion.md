# Finite, Evidence-Aware Setting Expansion

This standard governs the neighborhood-discovery frontend. The shared eight-axis, label, identity, evidence, bracket, and profile standards remain authoritative downstream. The expansion source is a proposed contract derived from Atlas settings, not a fabricated triggering paper.

## 1. Scope and versioned rounds

Freeze each request's source tasks, target tasks, seeds, allowed operators and axes, fixed constraints, cutoff date, priority mode, and budgets. Record the Atlas inventory used for discovery. A round may cover selected directions within that finite scope; it cannot claim to enumerate every possible problem in a field.

Use a compact, diverse seed portfolio. Explicit seed choices take precedence within the request. Unknown/missing tags are not evidence of a conflicting property. Keep unresolved, rejected, redirected, and deferred directions in the round ledger with reasons, so repeated runs can distinguish a new opportunity from unfinished work.

Default to one primary conceptual transformation. Necessary adjustments to multiple axes may belong to that transformation, but they must be declared and remain inside the authorized axes. A second independent change requires permission in the request and an explanation. Do not recursively seed from an unreviewed candidate; accepted additions can become seeds in a later round.

The requested number of profiles is a cap or target, not a completeness claim or a reason to weaken review. Exhausted budgets, inadequate evidence, and insufficient worthwhile candidates are legitimate stopping reasons.

## 2. Two distinct neighborhoods

**Discovery neighborhood:** a bounded collection of useful comparisons, such as the same task with a different native oracle, a related model on another object, or an approved cross-task transfer. Similar labels and graph proximity help choose material to read. They do not certify a reduction.

**Identity search:** the entire Atlas, using the shared high-recall retrieval and semantic comparison. A candidate generated from one local seed must still be checked against remote task names, aliases, interfaces, and representations. A small discovery radius must never become a hard exclusion in duplicate detection.

Keep relation semantics explicit: navigational similarity, model inclusion, algorithm-class inclusion, oracle simulation, target conversion, and equivalence are different claims. Mathematical edges require direction, parameter/quantifier conditions, and costs. A shared tag supports only a retrieval connection.

## 3. Opportunity cards before full specifications

The planner proposes a bounded set of short opportunity cards. Each names its seeds, target task, primary transformation, affected axes/objects, intended invariant conditions, expected value, possible transfer routes, and unresolved risks. The controller selects a diverse subset using the planner's recommendations and explicit caps. Scores organize work; they do not decide mathematical truth.

The expander then specifies selected directions individually. This avoids placing all seed profiles, all possible combinations, and all new contracts in one model request. Independent specification review and whole-Atlas identity comparison precede expensive bracket research.

Interpret priority modes as follows:

| Mode | Research priority | What the mode does not imply |
|---|---|---|
| Classical coverage | Foundational, reusable contracts missing from the current Atlas, with identifiable evidence routes | The setting must be a new research problem |
| Gaps | Meaningful comparisons whose closure is uncertain or plausibly incomplete, with explicit reasons to investigate | Missing Atlas evidence proves an open problem |
| Balanced | Coverage, mechanism diversity, likely research value, and manageable verification cost | Every candidate has equal value or equal cost |

Keep the selection explanation tied to the exact candidate. Name what becomes visible or difficult when the contract changes. Avoid generic claims of novelty, importance, or applicability.

## 4. Specification and well-posedness

Retain all eight axes: TASK, MODEL, ORACLE, ALGORITHMS, OUTPUT, METRIC, GUARANTEE, RESOURCE. The earlier expansion paradigm's ten-part checklist is an aid to completeness, not another competing schema:

- Task and hidden objects map to TASK and the ORACLE information register.
- Instance geometry, structural conditions, existence promises, and parameter domains map to MODEL.
- Interface, random/error mechanisms, public information, and access initialization map to ORACLE, with witness-specific initialization kept with the witness.
- Algorithm permissions map to ALGORITHMS and views.
- Output and error split between OUTPUT and METRIC.
- Success quantifiers map to GUARANTEE; actual operation costs and projections map to RESOURCE.
- Comparison parameters and quantifiers remain explicit in the appropriate model, guarantee, quantity, resource projection, and regime records.

The current profile/view granularity takes precedence over older examples: deterministic/randomized comparisons usually share a profile; a parameter endpoint usually belongs to a regime; different native oracles, model classes, or targets do not merge merely because their rates agree.

Check four matters before identity verification:

1. **Nonemptiness:** the defining assumptions and parameter ranges permit an instance.
2. **Defined objective/output:** solution selection, finiteness/existence promises, domains, and error are coherent.
3. **Coherent native information:** every legal query has the promised response/selection/noise semantics and explicit costs. Idealized exact information is permitted when modeled honestly; it does not assert cheap arithmetic implementation.
4. **Independent value:** the proposal exposes a useful contract or comparison and is not merely a renamed solved-by-definition case or an already parameterized endpoint.

Missing upper or lower bounds do not invalidate a complete research contract. Core unknowns that prevent anyone from deciding what an algorithm may observe or output do prevent formal identity confirmation.

## 5. Transfers that need special care

| Transformation | Required clarification |
|---|---|
| First/higher order to value-only | Retain the model deliberately; define legal value queries and domains; charge any derivative approximation or simulation. |
| Exact to noisy, or noisy to exact | Specify the population/sample object, noise law or adversarial class, conditioning, sample coupling/reuse, and precision costs; do not assume one is a parameter limit of the other. |
| Single level to bilevel | State which property moves to which function and variable block; define lower solution selection, outer target, every layer's primitive interface, and charged inner solves. |
| Scalar saddle to general VI | Check integrability/subclass restrictions and the direction of the claimed simulation; a general VI lower bound need not hold for scalar saddle operators. |
| Target/guarantee conversion | Give the error map, constants, parameter region, and quantifier/cost change; different losses or moments are not interchangeable labels. |
| Resource or boundary change | Preserve actual resource vectors before projection; determine whether the proposal changes the contract or only selects an existing regime. |

These are discovery directions, not automatic transfer rules. A theorem enters a target bracket only after the shared research/review chain verifies the original statement, applicability, and any necessary proof.

## 6. Provenance, review, and duplicates

Attach an `atlas_expansion` origin to a candidate, binding the frozen request, plan, opportunity, and seed snapshots. A newly selected assumption is a definition of the proposed contract. It must not be attributed to a paper that did not state it. Actual source claims retain their genuine evidence references.

The expansion reviewer checks scope, the full contract, well-posedness, value, tags, and every supplied pending peer. A verified batch duplicate is retained as an alias/provenance relationship to the pending canonical candidate, without duplicate research. Exact JSON fingerprints may suppress literal repetition; semantic equivalence remains a model judgment with stated reasons.

Pending-peer review does not establish whole-Atlas novelty. The shared matcher and independent identity reviewer decide existing/new/unresolved. An existing result may mean a new view or an updated regime inside a known profile. With existing updates disabled, report that route rather than manufacturing a new top-level node.

## 7. Research, human approval, and current-version checks

Confirmed new candidates enter the existing full profile research and independent review chain. Upper/lower witnesses, result cards, short proofs, applicability conversions, coverage obligations, bracket statuses, and the five native files follow the shared standards. Related profiles are leads; their claims are not adopted solely because they inspired the candidate.

The expansion frontend prepares a finite batch for the required human publication approval step. An independently reviewed draft is not an ingestion receipt. Before committing, the identity decision must still be valid for the current Atlas. Earlier additions in the same batch can invalidate the inventory version of later proposals; recheck identity and the affected bindings rather than changing a version string or removing the guard.

Reuse unchanged, formally recorded research where allowed, but do not inherit approval from a filename or stable bound ID. New matches may redirect or merge a pending candidate. A changed specification or affected evidence requires the corresponding review to run again.

Report separately the opportunities, specified candidates, duplicates, existing-setting redirects, unresolved identities, researched profiles, reviewed proposals awaiting approval, and actual receipts. New to the Atlas, new in the literature, and an unresolved complexity gap are three different conclusions.
